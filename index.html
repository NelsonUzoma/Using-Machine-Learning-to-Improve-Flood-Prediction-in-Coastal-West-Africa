    What this prototype is

    CoastGuard is the platform. FINN is the prediction engine inside it. This page shows the whole journey a warning takes, from environmental inputs to a plain-language message a community can act on.

    Real in this prototype

    • The dashboard, map, timeline and risk levels
    • Explanations of which factors drive each prediction
    • Alert writing, including a Pidgin preview
    • The demo mode for judges

    Simulated in this prototype

    • All rainfall, river, soil and tide numbers are synthetic scenarios
    • The risk score is a transparent weighted formula that stands in for the trained FINN model
    • Location values such as elevation are illustrative, not measured
    • Alert delivery and subscriber counts
    • No live data feeds and no real forecasts

    The pipeline

    IngestRainfall, river levels, elevation, soil moisture, land cover, satellite passes
    PredictFINN scores flood likelihood for each location and hour
    ExplainShow which factors pushed the score up
    TranslateTurn scores into Low, Moderate, High or Critical with actions
    DeliverSMS and dashboard alerts, later in local languages

    Risk levels

    • Low (under 25%): no action needed.
    • Moderate (25 to 50%): stay alert and clear drains.
    • High (50 to 75%): prepare now, move valuables up.
    • Critical (over 75%): move to higher ground or a shelter.

    Data sources to evaluate for the real build

    • Rainfall: CHIRPS and GPM IMERG
    • River discharge: GloFAS forecasts plus any local gauge records
    • Elevation: Copernicus DEM or SRTM
    • Soil moisture: SMAP or ESA CCI
    • Land cover: ESA WorldCover
    • Flood extent and history: Sentinel-1 imagery, Dartmouth Flood Observatory, national agency records

    Known limits to state openly

    • Sparse river gauges and patchy historical flood records in the region
    • Floods are rare events, so training data is heavily imbalanced
    • Predictions need validation with NEMA, NADMO and local partners before anyone relies on them
    • A neural network must beat simpler baselines such as XGBoost to earn its place
